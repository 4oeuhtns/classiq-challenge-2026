# Findings — 2026-09-17 · Scrutiny pass against the new rank-1 score (129 / 614 / 18)

New leaderboard entry: **Boopathi R., depth 129, CX 614, width 18, 2026-09-15 20:07 UTC**.
That is 614/129 = **4.76 CX per layer**, 52.9% of the 9-CX ceiling for 18 qubits.
Previous target was 137/561 = 4.09/layer. The new entry is *denser*, not just shorter.

This document re-audits the whole project against that number. Everything below is
measured, not argued. Nothing here is inherited from earlier phases — where an earlier
conclusion is contradicted, that is stated explicitly.

---

## 1. The depth gap is **not** a scheduling problem. That framing was wrong.

Phase 3.5 opened with a ratio argument ("CX only improved 2.2x while depth improved
14x — that is a parallel packing signature") and concluded: *chase parallelism
directly*. That inference does not survive a direct measurement.

A qubit can be touched at most **once per layer**. So for any gate list,
`depth >= max over qubits of (number of gates touching that qubit)`. That is a hard
floor no scheduler can beat. Measured on the current best circuit:

| | value |
|---|---:|
| transpiled depth | 1923 |
| CX | 1251 |
| u3 | 1658 |
| **max gate-touches on a single qubit** (q15 = `A[3]`) | **855** |

**A perfect scheduler cannot take our current gate list below depth 855.** The most
that scheduling can ever be worth to us is 1923 → 855, i.e. **2.2x**. We need 15x.
Phase 3.5 spent its whole budget on the wrong lever, and its negative result closed a
door that was never the door.

## 2. The real defect: 79% of all gate activity is scratch management

Gate-touches per qubit, current best circuit:

| qubit | role | touches | % of layers busy |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| q0–q5 | x data | 27–81 | 1.4–4.2% |
| q6–q11 | y data | 38–113 | 2.0–5.9% |
| q12 `A[0]` | y flag | 337 | 17.5% |
| q13 `A[1]` | scratch | 501 | 26.1% |
| q14 `A[2]` | scratch | 767 | 39.9% |
| q15 `A[3]` | scratch | **855** | 44.5% |
| q16 `A[4]` | scratch | 636 | 33.1% |
| q17 `A[5]` | scratch | 178 | 9.3% |

- **data total: 886 touches. ancilla total: 3274 touches.**
- The 12 qubits that hold the actual problem are idle **96%** of the time.
- Load is **30x unbalanced** (855 vs 27).

Now invert the leader's numbers. 614 CX = 1228 CX-touches over 18 qubits = **68.2
average**, and since touches-per-qubit ≤ depth, **no qubit exceeds 129**. The minimum
possible maximum is ⌈1228/18⌉ = 69. So their load is balanced to within at worst 1.9x,
against our 30x.

**A compute/uncompute AND-tree over a shared scratch pool cannot produce that profile.**
It structurally piles touches onto a few scratch qubits — that is what an AND-tree *is*.
This, not scheduling, is the architectural difference.

## 3. Where our CX actually go — the y-side is the whole problem

Per-piece breakdown of the current design (transpiled, `optimization_level=3`):

| stage | depth | CX | share of CX |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| y-flag compute + uncompute (10 pieces) | 1376 | 846 | **62%** |
| x-side MCZ (10 pieces) | 735 | 520 | 38% |

We spend **~85 CX to produce each one-bit y flag**, then throw it away, ten times.
The x-side — the part every previous phase tried to optimize (cubes, comparators,
octagons, XAG minimization, ILP cover) — is the *minority* of the cost. Phases 3, 3.5
and 3.6 were all aimed at the smaller half of the circuit.

## 4. Term count is already at its provable floor — so the gap is per-term cost

GF(2) rank of the mask is 10, which is a lower bound on the number of rank-1
(y-mask ⊗ x-mask) terms. `handbuilt.py` uses exactly 10. So:

- leader: 614 CX / 10 terms = **≤61 CX per rank-1 term**
- ours: 1366 CX model / 10 terms = **~137 CX per term**

Gap decomposes as **≈2.2x per-term cost × ≈6.6x load imbalance**. Both are real and both
must be fixed; neither alone gets there.

## 5. New structural fact: the logo has only **10 distinct nonzero rows**

Never computed before. This is much stronger than "rank 10".

| y-set | px | x-intervals | mirror sum |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| {11, 27} | 5 | [38,42] | 38 |
| {12, 26} | 9 | [36,44] | 38 |
| {13,14,24,25} | 13 | [34,46] | 38 |
| {15,16,22,23} | 15 | [33,47] | 38 |
| {17..21} | 17 | [32,48] | 38 |
| {29..34, 48..53} | 25 | [2,26] | 82 |
| {35, 47} | 30 | [2,26], [53,57] | 82 |
| {36, 46} | 34 | [2,26], [51,59] | 82 |
| {37,38,44,45} | 36 | [2,26], [50,60] | 82 |
| {39..43} | 60 | [2,61] | 82 |

Three facts fall out, all exact:

1. **13 x-intervals in total** describe all 1097 pixels. The whole logo is 10
   generalized rectangles (y-band × x-interval).
2. **Every y-band is perfectly mirror-symmetric** — `y + mirror(y)` is a single
   constant per band: 38 for the disk-2 bands (centre 19), 82 for all the rest
   (centre 41). No exceptions.
3. **The x-intervals are perfectly nested**, both stacks:
   [38,42] ⊂ [36,44] ⊂ [34,46] ⊂ [33,47] ⊂ [32,48] and
   [53,57] ⊂ [51,59] ⊂ [50,60] ⊂ (arm).

So both disks are exactly `|x − c| ≤ w(|y − c_y|)` with `w` a 9-entry table — which is
one 4-bit comparison against a small lookup, *not* five independent terms. The column
side is symmetric: 11 distinct column patterns.

## 6. Doors closed this session (all measured, all negative)

### 6.1 GF(2) linear basis change — closed, and the earlier test proved the wrong thing
Earlier work showed rank 10 is invariant under 300 random invertible GF(2) transforms
and concluded linear changes are useless. **Rank invariance is not the relevant
question** — rank bounds the *term count*, but our cost is *per-term*. The right
question is whether a basis change makes the individual masks cheaper as dyadic blocks.
Asked directly, with hill-climbing over 60 random restarts on the actual block-cost
model:

| register | base model CX | best found | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Y (flag, counted twice) | 1088 | 1088 | **1.00x** |
| X (MCZ) | 715 | 665 | 1.08x |
| combined | 1803 | 1753 | 1.03x |

The identity basis is already optimal on Y. Genuinely closed now, for the right reason.

### 6.2 Chaining the y-flag between pieces — 1.3x, confirmed
Instead of build/teardown per piece, evolve one flag by XOR-ing in the difference
between consecutive y-masks. Ordering matters, so it was solved **exactly** (held-Karp
TSP over all 10! orderings, returning to |0⟩):

- independent build/teardown: 1088 model CX
- optimal chained ordering: **855** model CX → only **1.3x**

Matches the earlier empirical note (`1918 → 2011 depth`). The pairwise XOR-differences
of the band partition are simply not small.

### 6.3 Threshold / staircase decomposition from the row structure — net worse
Using §5: `f = XOR_j [y ≥ t_j] · Δ_j` over the 20 row-change points, verified to
reconstruct the target exactly.

| | y-side | x-side | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| current pieces | 1088 | 715 | 1803 |
| threshold-chain | **756** (1.44x better) | **1572** (2.2x worse) | 2328 |

The y-side improves exactly as predicted — nested y-intervals are cheap. The x-side
collapses because each Δ is a **thin mirror-symmetric annulus** (popcounts 2/4/5, e.g.
{36,37,43,44}) which shatters into dyadic singletons. The decomposition is right; the
*coordinate system* is wrong.

## 7. What this points at

The three measurements agree on one thing. §6.3 says the staircase structure is correct
but dies on mirror-symmetric annuli. §5 says every band is exactly mirror-symmetric and
every x-stack is exactly nested. §2 says the winning circuit must have near-uniform
qubit load, which rules out per-term AND-tree scratch churn.

All three are solved by the same move: **fold the coordinates once, globally, and pay
for it once.** Compute `|y − 41|`, `|y − 19|`, `|x − 40|`, `|x − 55|`-style folded
values into ancilla at the top of the circuit, reuse them across all 10 terms, unfold
at the end. In folded coordinates every one of the 10 terms becomes a monotone
threshold — and thresholds on a shared folded register are exactly the kind of gate
that touches data qubits directly and spreads load evenly.

**This is not what the octagon experiment tested.** That experiment (1361/967 for
*one* disk) built the arithmetic *per shape*, from scratch, with a hand-written
O(n)-depth ripple adder, and never reused it. The untested version is: fold once,
share across all ten terms, and use a parallel-prefix comparator rather than a ripple
chain. The per-term budget that has to be beaten is **61 CX**.

Secondary, independent of the above: the y-side is 62% of the circuit and every
previous phase optimized the x-side. Even without folding, any y-side win is worth
1.6x more than the same win on x.

## 8. The fold was built and measured. It is dead — by ~4x, on depth.

Built `y -> |y-c|` in place: constant subtract mod 64 (incrementer cascade), threshold
compare into a sign ancilla, conditional bitwise NOT, controlled increment. Verified by
exhaustive simulation over all 64 inputs: `|y-c|` exact, scratch returns clean,
`(sign, d)` injective.

| fold | depth | CX | fold + unfold |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| about c=19 | 239 | 145 | **478 / 290** |
| about c=41 | 253 | 156 | **506 / 312** |

**One fold+unfold is 3.7x the leader's entire depth budget** (478 vs 129) and 47% of
their entire CX budget — before a single term is evaluated. The design needs four folds
(y@19, y@41, x@40, x@55), so ~1200 CX / ~1950 depth of pure preamble.

**Why, structurally:** `|y-c|` is not GF(2)-affine (§6.1 already showed no linear map
captures the mirror — reflection about an integer centre is provably not an XOR). It
therefore requires a borrow/carry chain plus a conditional two's-complement negate. A
carry chain is a serial Toffoli cascade, which is the one thing the depth budget cannot
absorb. See §9 for why even a depth-optimal parallel-prefix rewrite does not rescue it.

## 9. The budget arithmetic — this is the constraint everything must satisfy

Measured, transpiled in isolation to u3/cx:

| gate | depth | CX |
|---|---:|---:|
| `rccx` (relative-phase Toffoli) | **7** | 3 |
| `ccx` (full Toffoli) | 11 | 6 |

So **depth 129 is at most 18 sequential relative-phase Toffoli levels for the entire
oracle.** With 18 qubits at most 6 Toffolis run concurrently.

Let T = relative-phase Toffolis, L = bare CX, so `3T + L = 614`. Under *perfect*
packing, `depth >= 7T/6 + L/9`:

- taking the measured isolated Toffoli depth of 7 → **T ≤ 73**
- generously assuming every u3 packs for free, so a Toffoli level costs only its 3 CX
  plus one layer (4) → **T ≤ 182**

Even the generous bound is 182 Toffolis for all 1097 pixels. Against that:

- our y-side alone (ten one-bit flags) is 846 CX ≈ **282 Toffolis**
- mockturtle's XAG minimisation of the bounding-box-reduced **disk 1 alone** found
  **248 AND gates** (Phase 3 strategy D). That is a heuristic result, so it bounds the
  minimum from above, not below — but it is the best any real minimiser we ran produced,
  for one shape out of four.

**A design dominated by high-arity AND trees cannot fit in this budget.** That is not a
statement about our particular decomposition; it applies to cubes, comparators, octagon
arithmetic, XAG, ILP cover and pebbling equally, because all of them are AND-based.

### 9.1 Two new basis measurements

Both are ancilla-free representations, computed directly on the 4096-entry truth table:

| basis | nonzero terms | cost if built term-by-term |
|---|---:|---|
| Walsh / parity (as already known) | 4096 / 4096 | ~4095 CX, depth 2ⁿ — dead |
| **ANF / monomial (never computed before)** | **886 / 4096** | 26,146 CX — dead |

The ANF is genuinely sparser than a random function (~2048 expected), confirming real
structure, but its monomials are concentrated at degree 6–9, and a degree-k monomial is
a C^(k-1)Z. Useless directly; recorded so nobody recomputes it.

### 9.2 Ancilla-free rotation gates — measured, wins only at k=3

`(-1)^(x1...xk)` can be built with 2^k-1 rotations on parities, using **zero ancilla and
zero Toffolis**, so such gates have no scratch contention and pack up to 9 CX/layer.
Measured against our AND-tree `MCZ`:

| k | AND-tree MCZ (k-2 ancilla) | ancilla-free `mcp(pi)` |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | depth 15, cx 7 | **depth 12, cx 6** |
| 4 | **depth 23, cx 13** | depth 33, cx 20 |
| 5 | **depth 27, cx 19** | depth 68, cx 44 |
| 6 | **depth 35, cx 25** | depth 130, cx 84 |
| 7 | **depth 35, cx 31** | depth 192, cx 140 |

The 2^k blowup kills it above k=3. So "it must be all rotations" is **not** established —
that family fails too, at exactly the arities our x-side blocks use (k up to 7).

### 9.3 What §9 actually leaves standing

The budget admits ~73–182 Toffolis total, 6-wide, 18 levels deep. Two things follow, and
they are the only surviving direction:

1. **Arity must come down.** Ancilla-free rotation gates beat AND trees at k=3 and lose
   from k=4. Our blocks run k=4–7. A decomposition into many *degree-≤3* terms would be
   both cheaper per term and free of scratch contention — which is the only mechanism
   seen so far that produces balanced qubit load (§2).
2. **The y-side must get ~4x cheaper**, since at 282 Toffolis it alone overruns the
   generous budget. §6 closed chaining (1.3x), linear basis (1.00x) and the staircase
   (net worse); §8 closed folding. What has *not* been tried is minimising each 6-variable
   mask as an exact low-arity XAG — mockturtle was only ever run on the 10-variable
   reduced disks, never on the 6-variable masks where exact synthesis is tractable.

## 10. The Toffoli budget, derived properly (supersedes §9's estimate)

Measured packing of relative-phase Toffolis on 18 qubits (6 disjoint triples per level):

| levels | rccx | depth | layers per level |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 6 | 7 | 7.00 |
| 4 | 24 | 25 | 6.25 |
| 16 | 96 | 97 | **6.06** |

**Correction, 2026-09-17 (later).** This section originally charged a Toffoli
**18** qubit-layers, on the grounds that it occupies 3 qubits for 6 layers. That is
wrong: its two controls are idle most of those layers. Measured directly on the
96-rccx stack above — 288 CX + 384 u3 = 960 qubit-touches against 18 x 97 = 1746
available — a Toffoli stack runs at **55% occupancy**, so a Toffoli really costs
**10 qubit-layers, and 45% of the stack is free for other gates**. The qubit-layer
bound is therefore only `10T + 2L <= 2322`, giving `T <= 274` — much weaker than
claimed.

The binding constraint is **depth**, not qubit-layers. Toffolis pack 6-wide at 6
layers per level, so T Toffolis cost ~T layers; bare CX pack 9-wide, so L of them cost
L/9 layers. With `3T + L = 614`:

```
T + L/9 = 129   and   3T + L = 614   ->   T = 91,  L = 340
```

**~91 relative-phase Toffolis and ~340 bare CX.** The number is unchanged; the
derivation behind it now is. Confirmed constructively: 21 super-layers of 3 Toffolis
on 9 qubits with the other 9 qubits running a rotating CX ladder gives **depth 127,
573 CX, 4.51 CX/layer** — the leader's profile is reachable.

**Structure is required, not just gate count.** Random placement of the same gate mix
never exceeded **3.6 CX/layer** and never got below depth 165 at 614 CX (8 mixes
measured, T from 40 to 200). Density of 4.76 is not what you get from "lots of
independent gates"; it needs deliberate co-scheduling of Toffolis and CX on
complementary qubit sets.

### 10.1 The density signature points at XOR, not rotations

A *densely packed pure-Toffoli stack* runs at **288 CX / 97 depth = 2.97 CX per layer**
(measured above). The leader is at **4.76** — materially *higher* than an all-Toffoli
circuit can reach. Their circuit therefore cannot be Toffoli-dominated: it must be
Toffoli-sparse with bare CX filling the idle qubit-layers. Bare CX = XOR. That is
exactly the signature of an **XAG** (AND/XOR graph) implemented as compute-Z-uncompute,
and it also explains the balanced load of §2, since XORs touch data qubits directly and
pack 9 to a layer.

## 12. Where this actually lands

The budget and the best available synthesis give a single, concrete, checkable target:

> **An XAG for the logo with at most ~45 AND gates** (2 x 45 = 90 Toffolis, just inside
> the ceiling of 91), with the remaining ~340 CX spent on XORs.

Best achieved by any tool we have: **407** joint, **208** decomposed. The gap is
**4.6x–9x in multiplicative complexity**, and that single number is now the whole
problem. Everything else measured this session — folding (§8), chaining (§6.2), linear
basis (§6.1), the staircase (§6.3), ancilla-free rotations (§9.2), the ANF basis (§9.1) —
is closed.

For calibration: the worst-case multiplicative complexity of *any* 12-variable Boolean
function is 120 (Boyar–Peralta–Pochuev), so ~45 is not obviously out of reach for a
function this structured — but it is far below what `cut_rewriting` with 4-input cuts
can find. The tool, not the idea, is the current limit.

## 13. SAT-based exact synthesis — and why "minimise AND" is the wrong objective

Built a Boyar–Peralta style SAT encoding (`experiments/mc_exact.py`): signal 0 is the
constant 1, signals 1..n the inputs, signal n+1+i the output of AND gate i whose two
inputs are each an XOR of earlier signals. The subsets are the SAT variables; per
assignment values are Tseitin-encoded. Two things make it run: **CEGAR** (constraining
only a subset of the 2ⁿ assignments is a relaxation, so a model is checked by
simulation and a mismatch is added) and a **wall-clock bound per g**, chunked by
conflict budget so a hard step yields rather than hangs.

Validated against every published multiplicative-complexity value it could be checked
against, with each returned chain re-simulated to confirm it computes the function:

| function | known MC | found |
|---|---:|---:|
| x0 ⊕ x1 | 0 | 0 |
| x0 ∧ x1 | 1 | 1 |
| MAJ3 | 1 | 1 |
| AND3 / AND4 / AND5 / AND6 | 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 | 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 |

### 13.1 It minimises AND, and that makes the circuit worse

| mask | mockturtle (iterated) | SAT min-AND |
|---|---|---|
| y0 | 12 AND, 3 XOR | **5 AND**, 41 XOR |
| x0 | 9 AND, 0 XOR | **6 AND**, 75 XOR |

The AND counts are 2.0–2.4x better and are genuinely closer to optimal. But the XOR
counts explode, because nothing in the encoding penalises them — and in the built
circuit a chain is applied *four* times (fold and unfold per gate inside the forward
pass, then the whole pass inverted to clean the ancilla). Costing one forward pass at
`3g` CX for the AND gates plus `2x` CX for the folds:

- x0 via mockturtle: 9 AND, 0 XOR → ~27 CX
- x0 via SAT min-AND: 6 AND, 75 XOR → ~168 CX

**So §9–§12's framing was too narrow.** Those sections treat AND gates as the scarce
resource because a Toffoli is 3 CX and ~6 layers against a CX's 1 and 1. That ratio is
right, but it does not follow that the AND count is the thing to minimise: an
MC-optimal chain buys its low AND count with so many XORs that it loses outright. The
Toffoli *ceiling* of ~91 (§10) still stands — it is a resource bound and does not
depend on the objective — but "get the AND count to ~45" is not by itself the goal.

The objective that matters is the cost the circuit actually pays, `3*AND + 2*XOR` per
forward pass. `experiments/mc_cost.py` searches candidate (AND, XOR) pairs in
increasing order of that cost, using a cardinality bound on the total subset size, and
returns the first feasible one — which is optimal for this cost model rather than for
either count alone.

### 13.2 The XAG chain form hits the same 6-ancilla wall

`experiments/xag_circuit.py` builds a chain into a reversible circuit. The XOR parts
are linear, so they fold onto a pivot qubit in place and need **zero scratch** — which
is the whole structural appeal, since the AND-tree design spends 79% of its gate
activity on scratch (§2). But that trade has a price: **a chain needs one ancilla per
AND gate**, held live until the gate is consumed.

We have 6 ancilla, one of which holds the piece's flag, so **at most 5 AND gates**.
The cost-optimal chains want 8. Running the cost-aware search capped at `g <= 5` and
measuring the built circuit against the dyadic-block version of the same y-flag
(builder verified exhaustively over all 64 values of y: correct flag, clean scratch):

| y-mask | dyadic blocks (depth/CX) | SAT XAG chain | AND/XOR | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| y0 | 66 / 39 | *no chain at g ≤ 5* | — | doesn't fit |
| y7 | 91 / 54 | *no chain at g ≤ 5* | — | doesn't fit |
| y4 | 49 / 33 | 45 / 37 | 5 / 3 | depth −8%, CX +12% |
| y9 | 67 / 41 | **41 / 29** | 4 / 2 | depth −39%, CX −29% |

So where it fits it is worth between nothing and 1.6x on depth, and on the two hardest
masks it does not fit at all. This is the **same wall that killed caterpillar**
(findings-2026-09-15 §10, which could not find a pebbling schedule at any budget from
6 up to 36–38 pebbles): representations that trade scratch for held intermediates all
run out of the 6 ancilla, and the only escape is recomputation, which costs back the
depth it was meant to save.

### 13.3 Verdict

SAT exact synthesis did what it was asked to do — it proved mockturtle was leaving
~2x on the AND count, and gave a validated tool for the question. But it does not
change the outcome:

1. AND count was never what the circuit pays; minimising it makes the circuit worse.
2. Minimising real cost gives at best ~1.6x on the largest line item, and nothing at
   all on the masks that cost the most.
3. The ~91-Toffoli ceiling (§10) versus our ~455 is a 5x shortfall that a 1.6x local
   improvement on part of the y-side does not touch.

The rank-1-per-piece architecture is now closed from both ends: §11.1 showed no
synthesis of its 20 masks fits the budget, and §13.2 shows the representation that
would make its masks cheap does not fit in 6 ancilla. **The leaders are not building
this architecture.** Anything further should start from a different decomposition, not
a better synthesis of this one.

## 14. Nested-shell oracle — a new best, 1738 / 1171 (`experiments/shells.py`)

Built the architecture §12 pointed at. The ten distinct nonzero rows form **two chains
under inclusion** — disk 2's five, and the stem/disk-1/arm five — so for each chain
`row(y) = P_level(y)` with `P_1 ⊂ P_2 ⊂ ... ⊂ P_5`, and with `S_j = P_j \ P_{j-1}`:

```
f(x,y) = XOR_j  [level(y) >= j] * S_j(x)
```

Verified against TARGET exactly. The point is the y side: `[level >= j]` is a *nested*
family, so **one flag ancilla walks down the chain**, XOR-ing in one band per step,
instead of ten independent masks each built and discarded.

**Result: depth 1738, cx 1171** (phase error 1.6e-17, zero ancilla leak), against
1923 / 1251. Better on both, so lexicographically a new best.

### 14.1 Where the gain came from, with controls

| build | depth | CX |
|---|---:|---:|
| handbuilt, dyadic blocks (previous best) | 1923 | 1251 |
| handbuilt + cube covers only | 1871 | 1248 |
| nested shells + cubes, clear between chains | 1748 | 1178 |
| nested shells + cubes, chains carried across | 1791 | 1189 |
| **nested shells + cubes, best walk order** | **1738** | **1171** |

So the architecture is worth more than the cube covers (1871 → 1748 vs 1923 → 1871),
and the two are roughly additive. Three things that had to be got right:

- **Chain splitting must be geometric, not greedy.** First-fit puts the full-width arm
  row [2,61] on disk 2's chain (it is a superset of [32,48] as well as of the stem),
  giving chains of 6 and 4 and a large scattered shell: 1632 model CX against 1573.
- **Cubes, not dyadic blocks, for the shells.** The shells are thin mirror-symmetric
  annuli; {32,48} differs in one bit so it is a single 5-control cube (25 CX) where
  dyadic blocks need two 6-control singletons (62 CX). Exact minimum-cost cover over
  all 3⁶ cubes, up to four of them, by meet-in-the-middle (`experiments/cubes.py`).
- **The chained flag needs exact Toffolis.** The flag is not uncomputed by a literal
  inverse, so an `rccx` would leave a control-dependent relative phase that never
  cancels — the silent-wrong-answer trap in handbuilt.py's docstring. `MCX_exact`
  spends 6 CX on the final write instead of 3. Chaining still wins despite that.
- Carrying the flag across the two chains **loses** (1791 vs 1738): the mask separating
  them is large and scattered, so the transition costs more than a fresh start.

### 14.2 It does not change the strategic picture

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| ancilla share of all gate activity | 79% | **76%** |
| max touches on one qubit (hard depth floor) | 855 | **768** |

Still ~1.4x off its own scheduling floor and **13x off the leader's 129**. The y-side
was fixed roughly as predicted, but the x-side still applies each shell as an AND-tree
MCZ against the shared scratch pool, so the dominant defect of §2 survives. The next
target is the x-side, which is now the majority of the remaining cost.

## 15. The x-side — relabel the register. New best **1561 / 1068**

### 15.1 Two things that did not work, checked first

- **Transpose.** The target has 11 distinct *columns* as well as 10 distinct rows, so
  the same construction should run with the axes swapped. It does not: under the
  geometric split the columns come out as chains of 1 and 9 and **are not nested**, so
  there is no column-shell decomposition to build.
- **Grouping levels (`experiments/grouped.py`).** shells.py and handbuilt.py are the
  two endpoints of one family — chaining gives cheap nested y-thresholds but expensive
  annular *shells*, while bands give expensive y but cheap *full sets*. Partitioning a
  chain's levels into consecutive groups interpolates between them, and all 2⁴
  groupings per chain were searched. **Full chaining `[(1,5)]` is already optimal for
  both chains**, before and after the relabelling below. That trade is closed.

### 15.2 What worked: a GF(2)-linear relabelling of the register

The shells are thin mirror-symmetric pairs about the disk centres. A pair is a single
cheap cube exactly when its two elements differ in **one** bit — {32,48} does, {33,47}
differs in three and costs two 6-control singletons instead. Which bits a pair differs
in is not fixed: since `L(u) XOR L(v) = L(u XOR v)`, a linear relabelling of the
register chooses it.

Hill-climbing over invertible 6x6 GF(2) matrices, scoring with an exact ≤2-cube table
(O(1) lookup, which is what makes the search affordable), then re-costing the winner
with the full ≤4-cube cover:

| register | model cost before | after | relabel cost (applied + inverted) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| X (shells) | 689 | **496** | 14 CX |
| Y (bands) | 646 | 604 | 16 CX |

The relabelling commutes with the entire opposite side — the y work never touches X —
and the two networks act on disjoint registers, so they run in parallel.

### 15.3 Measured, cumulative

| build | depth | CX |
|---|---:|---:|
| handbuilt.py, dyadic blocks | 1923 | 1251 |
| + exact cube covers | 1871 | 1248 |
| + nested-shell chaining of the y flag | 1738 | 1171 |
| + X relabelled | 1569 | 1078 |
| **+ Y relabelled** (`experiments/best_oracle.py`) | **1561** | **1068** |

Phase error 1.6e-17, zero ancilla leak. **1.23x depth and 1.17x CX** over the previous
best, and better on both metrics so lexicographically a clean improvement.

### 15.4 Why this still is not the answer

| | handbuilt | now |
|---|---:|---:|
| ancilla share of gate activity | 79% | **75%** |
| max touches on one qubit (hard depth floor) | 855 | **705** |
| CX per layer | 0.65 | 0.69 |

Every gain so far has been a *cheaper decomposition*, and the density has barely moved:
0.65 → 0.69 CX per layer against the leader's 4.76. Three quarters of the gate activity
is still scratch management on a 6-ancilla pool, which is the defect §2 identified and
none of §14–15 touches. Getting from 1561 to 129 is not more of this — it needs an
architecture whose gates are not AND-trees queued on shared scratch.

## 16. Different path: a phase polynomial. No AND trees in the marking at all

Everything in §14–15 was compute → Z → uncompute, and §15.4 said the gains were all
cheaper *decompositions* while the density barely moved. So drop the premise.

A diagonal unitary over `{u3, cx}` **is** a phase polynomial: one `p(θ)` per nonzero
Walsh coefficient of the phase function, on a parity assembled by CNOTs. No AND
gates, no scratch, no compute/uncompute. The reason this was never on the table is
that the count is the Walsh support of `π·f`, and for a 12-bit function that is
generically 4096. Measured on the actual target: **4096 of 4096.** Completely dense.
Ancilla-free, this is dead — and the support size is invariant under GF(2)-linear
relabelling, so no change of basis rescues it. That part of §6 stands.

### 16.1 The ancilla are not scratch, they are a coordinate

The target has **11 distinct columns**, so the x-axis partitions into 10 nonzero
classes and

    f(x,y) = XOR_k [x in class k] · col_k(y)

Hold `col_k(y)` — the colour of class k at the current row — in an ancilla, and the
phase `π Σ_k a_k(x) q_k` is **linear in the ancilla**. That is the whole trick. For a
phase function linear in the code the Walsh support collapses onto the code-parities
`{0, e_1, …, e_r}` instead of all `2^r`, and the gate count becomes

    Σ_k |supp(â_k)| + |supp(union)|  =  450 + 64  =  514

Measured against the alternatives, on the same 6 ancilla:

| code on y | Rz gates | active code-parities |
|---|---:|---:|
| none (12 qubits, ancilla-free) | 4096 | 64 of 64 |
| nonlinear 4-bit row code | 1024 | 16 of 16 |
| nonlinear 5-bit row code | 2048 | 32 of 32 |
| **linear, two passes** | **530** | **6 + 6** |
| floor (sum of column supports) | 524 | — |

**Linearity, not compression, is what buys the sparsity.** The nonlinear code
compresses 64 rows to 11 just as well and costs twice as much, because every one of
its `2^m` code-parities stays active. Proof it cannot do better: the 10 nonzero rows
are R-independent, so no signed combination of them vanishes unless rows repeat, and
repeating enough of them to kill a parity makes the table translation-invariant —
i.e. a smaller code. So a nonlinear m-bit code always has all `2^m` active.

### 16.2 What actually got built

`experiments/phasepoly.py`. Rank is 10 and there are 6 ancilla, so the columns cannot
all be live; instead an ancilla walks a **closed tour** of columns, stepping from
`col_j` to `col_k` by XOR-ing in their difference (exact Held–Karp over the 11 nodes).
K such tours run at once: stepping a column touches y and scratch, walking parities
touches x and the accumulator, and those are disjoint.

| | depth | cx |
|---|---:|---:|
| rebuild each column (first working version) | 2381 | 1567 |
| + chained encoder (1532 → 640 cx) | 1344 | 1201 |
| + cube covers, piece-weighted tour | **1318** | **1243** |
| best AND-tree design (§15) | 1561 | 1068 |

Verified exact: phase error 3.2e-16, ancilla leak 0. **1318 / 1243 beats 1561 / 1068
on the primary metric.** CX is 1.16× worse, which the lexicographic score does not care
about, but it is the honest cost.

### 16.3 Three things that only hold in this architecture

* **Relative-phase Toffolis are safe — until you chain.** With a literal inverse,
  `E = D·P` gives `E† M E = P† M P` for any diagonal M, so rccx costs nothing in
  correctness. Chaining removes the literal inverse and the residue survives; measured
  it directly (it depends on the controls *and* the target). But the encoder reads
  only y and returns its ancilla to `|0⟩`, so the entire debt is **a function of y
  alone** — paid by one diagonal on the otherwise-idle y register rather than by exact
  Toffolis on every write. This is §14's silent-wrong-answer trap, with a cheaper exit.

* **Parallel accumulators must disagree.** Two chains generated by the same greedy
  Gray rule step the *same x qubit on the same layer* and collide every time: walks
  measured 990 deep for one chain and 931 for two — no parallelism at all. Rotating
  the tie-break per accumulator is the fix.

* **The x register is only ever a control.** Nothing queues behind shared scratch;
  the walk runs at one CX per phase gate (518 cx for 472 gates).

### 16.4 Where the bottleneck is now, and what is closed

It moved. It is no longer scratch churn — it is the **encoder's piece count**: each
cover piece is an AND tree plus its inverse landing on the same accumulator, so pieces
cannot overlap. Encoder-only depth 938 for 39 pieces ≈ 24 layers per piece.

Closed by measurement:
* **Y relabelling to widen blocks** — searched; max control count stays 6 (some row is
  always isolated), so scratch need stays 3 and K stays capped at 2. No gain.
* **Replica accumulators** — worse (2094 vs 1791). The replicas are scratch qubits, so
  copying into them serialises against the next encode step; the overlap it destroys is
  worth more than the parallelism it buys. Dedicated partners that the encoder never
  touches: also worse (1755 vs 1716), because at K=1 walk and encode alternate on the
  same accumulator anyway.
* **Basis choice (GL(10,F₂))** — the x side alone is a binary matroid, so greedy is
  *exact*: 472 → 316 phase gates. But `M = N^-T` pins the y side, and the x-optimal
  basis costs 1660 cx to encode. Joint hill-climb: 3144 → 2904 on `2·tour + 3.2·p`, ~8%.
  Real but not the lever.
* **K = 3** — a recursive-split AND (one recomputation for room) drops a 6-control
  block from 4 scratch to 3, so K=3 builds. It loses: the third tour costs more encoder
  than the third accumulator saves (1732 vs 1318).

### 16.5 Honest read

The depth floor for this design, if it packed perfectly over 18 qubits, is about
`(2·cx + p)/18 ≈ 160`. It measures 1318, so ~8× off ideal packing, and the gap is
almost entirely the serial encoder. That is a *specific, local* defect with known
attacks (sharing AND-tree prefixes across pieces of one cover; a proper parallel
parity network that uses x qubits as accumulators, which would take 12 accumulators
instead of 2). Unlike §15.4 — where the criticism was that the whole approach had no
path to the target — this one has an identified path and a computed floor near the
leader's 129.


## 17. Breadth sweep over the decomposition space (before optimising anything)

§16.5 said the floor of the phase-polynomial design was ~160. **That was wrong as
stated** — it is `(2·cx + p)/18` for the gate list I happened to have, i.e. a perfect
packing bound on one circuit, not a bound on the architecture. Corrected below.

Every design in this family is `f = XOR_k a_k(u) b_k(v)`, and exactly two numbers
decide it: the phase-gate count `Σ_k |supp(â_k)|` (accumulator load) and the closed
TSP tour through the `b_k` masks (encoder). Both are computable without building
anything, so the space was swept analytically first.

| decomposition | terms | phase gates | encoder | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| columns (the §16 design) | 10 | 488 | 640 cx / 35 pieces | baseline |
| **rows — the transpose** | **10** | **458** | **582 cx / 34 pieces** | **won** |
| columns, matroid-optimal basis | 10 | 380 | 823 cx | net worse |
| rows, matroid-optimal basis | 10 | 399 | 926 cx | net worse |
| geometric inclusion–exclusion | 13 | 652 | 609 cx | worse |
| target XOR a cheap rectangle | 11 | 492 | 667 cx | no help |
| dyadic half-split, either axis | 11 | 482–504 | 539–582 cx | worse |

### 17.1 The transpose — new best **1105 / 1122**

The only change is which register the ancilla encode. Rows beat columns on *both*
numbers, and it compounds: depth 1318 → **1105**, cx 1243 → **1122**. Verified exact
(phase error 4.8e-16, ancilla leak 0). Gate mix: 633 cx, 505 p, 194 rccx, 139 x.

This is the kind of thing worth checking before optimising: it cost one flag and beat
every structural idea in the table.

### 17.2 Closed by measurement

* **Geometric decomposition.** The logo is `R1 ∨ R2 ∨ D1 ∨ D2`; the two disks are
  disjoint and only `R1∩R2` and `R2∩D1` are non-empty, so inclusion–exclusion mod 2
  gives an exact 6-set form (verified). Four of the six are rank 1. But it needs 13
  terms against 10, and 652 phase gates against 458 — the column/row form is already
  rank-optimal and the geometric one is not.
* **XOR a cheap set off first.** A rank-1 rectangle can only merge line-classes if it
  spans them exactly; searched over aligned rectangles, best was 492 vs 488. Nothing.
* **Dyadic pre-split.** Fewer distinct lines per half, but the straddling lines get
  duplicated and the masks still need 6 controls. Worse on both.
* **Per-class hybrid (walk vs MCZ).** Terms just add, so each class can pick. Only 2
  of 10 prefer an MCZ, worth 418 → 397 cx (5%) — and an MCZ needs AND-tree scratch,
  which is the exact resource the encoder is already starved of. Not taken.
* **Matroid basis.** Greedy is *exact* for the x side (binary matroid), giving the
  true phase-gate floor: **316** for columns, **335** for rows. But `M = N^-T` pins
  the other side and the encoder more than eats the gain. Confirms §16.4.

### 17.3 What this family can and cannot reach

Phase-gate floor is exact (316 / 335). For the encoder, a tour is 11 edges and every
edge is a non-zero element of the span, the cheapest of which costs 21 cx — so

    rigorous floor  >=  335 + 11 x 21  =  566 cx

The **realistic** floor is far higher (~900): the basis that minimises phase gates has
a 926 cx encoder, and the basis that minimises the encoder gives back almost no phase
gates. So the leader's **614 cx is probably out of reach for this family, but not
provably so** — I cannot close that gap with what I have.

That matters for strategy: 614 cx = 91 Toffolis x 6 + 68 bare is *exactly* the budget
§10 derived from depth 129. The leader is most likely Toffoli-based with excellent
scheduling, not a phase polynomial. This family's advantage is depth per unit of
correctness work, not CX — and depth is scored first.


## 18. Depth-only: **998** (`experiments/phasepoly.py`), and a search to run

With CX declared free, the objective changes and so do the answers. Four things moved
the number; two plausible ones did not.

| step | depth | cx |
|---|---:|---:|
| §17 transposed baseline | 1105 | 1122 |
| + list-scheduled phases (replacing the fixed Gray walk) | 1095 | 1126 |
| + covers costed by piece **depth**, not CX | 1095 | 1135 |
| + relabelling of the encoded register | 1034 | 1055 |
| + **walk hosts dedicated outside the scratch pool** | **998** | **1213** |

Verified exact: phase error 4.8e-16, ancilla leak 0.

### 18.1 Six ancilla, three jobs

The whole design is one trade. The ancilla have to be tour accumulators, AND-tree
scratch, *and* walk hosts, and every gain in one starves another:

* **Hosts divide the walk.** A lone accumulator alternates cx, p, cx, p on one qubit,
  so its walk is exactly as deep as it is long. A list-scheduler over h hosts (each
  carrying its own running parity, at most one per register bit per layer) takes the
  walk from 900 layers to 487 / 357 / 300 / 240 at h = 2 / 3 / 4 / 6. Beyond six it
  stops helping — there are only six register bits, so six controls per layer, full
  stop.
* **But hosts have to come from somewhere.** Taking them from the scratch pool makes
  each walk a dependency of the next chain's encode step and *loses* (1227 vs 1034).
  Dedicating ancilla the encoder never touches wins (998). That was the difference
  between the scheduler being worthless and being worth 36 depth.
* **Scratch is set by the widest cover piece.** A 6-control piece wants 3 scratch, so
  with K=1 only 2 hosts are left. A relabelling that narrowed every mask to 2 scratch
  would buy a third host for free — which is why the search now penalises it.

### 18.2 What the search space actually looks like

Random invertible `L` and `N` are **all far worse** than the identity — sampled
candidates measured 2063 to 6356 against 1095 — because the nested chain structure
that makes the tour cheap is fragile. The correlation between the cheap model and
true depth was only +0.49. So annealing that jumps is worse than useless here; the
thing that worked was plain steepest descent from the identity, which took the
encoder's tour depth 1094 → 835 on its first restart.

`experiments/depthsearch.py` is built around that finding: basin-hopping descent, a
few row-operations from the incumbent, never a random matrix. Its inner objective is
the tour's piece depth, which is **exact** for the encoder and independent of the
phase side (`L` never touches the walked register) plus a scratch penalty. Every
improvement is built, verified by exact statevector, and transpiled before being
recorded, so a modelling error can waste time but cannot produce a wrong answer.
Checkpointed to `depthsearch_best.json` and resumable.

    python depthsearch.py 8        # hours, optional seed as second arg

### 18.3 Where it stands

Split now: encoder 589, walks ~357, total 998 — so at K=1 they barely overlap. The
obvious next win is getting the overlap back, which needs a relabelling whose masks
need only 2 scratch so that K=2 can still afford dedicated hosts. That is exactly what
the search is pointed at.

Honest ceiling: this architecture looks like it bottoms out somewhere near 600, not
129. §17.3 already found its CX floor sits above the leader's 614, and 614 = 91
Toffolis x 6 + 68 bare is precisely the budget §10 derived from depth 129. The leader
is near-certainly Toffoli-based with very aggressive scheduling. Getting to 129 is
likely a different architecture again — but 998 is the shallowest thing measured here,
against 1561 for everything that came before.
