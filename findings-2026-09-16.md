# Session Findings — 2026-09-16: Literature Research and a Careful Sanity Pass

*A companion to `plan.md` and `findings-2026-09-15.md`, not a replacement for either.
That earlier document covers a day of hand-built experiments (disks, octagon,
comparators) and closes with a research summary. This document picks up from
there: a broader literature search, and — because the first pass turned up one
lead that looked very exciting — a deliberately skeptical second pass over every
claim before anything gets built. Where a claim couldn't be verified, that's
written down as plainly as a claim that was verified. The lesson of this whole
project so far is "measure, don't guess," and that applies to papers too, not just
our own circuits.*

---

## 0. The one-paragraph summary

A broad web search turned up several real, recent papers that looked like they might
explain the leaderboard's 137/561 result. On a careful second read — quoting exact
theorem text instead of trusting a first summary — the most exciting-looking one does
not hold up: its own proof does not explain the depth bound it claims. A second
promising-looking tool, on reflection against what this project already knows
(Findings 1 and 8 from the previous day), turns out to likely just be a more
optimal version of an approach already tried and found wanting. What's left
standing is smaller than the first pass suggested, but it is real, and it points to
one specific, genuinely novel technique — borrowing the coordinate qubits themselves
as temporary scratch space — that has never been tried in this project and doesn't
depend on trusting any paper's unverified claims to test.

---

## 1. Why a second, skeptical pass was necessary

Earlier the same day, an AI-summarized read of a graph-coloring paper produced a
specific, confident-sounding claim ("qubit relabeling and ancilla allocation convert
gates to commuting forms") that a cleaner re-fetch of the same paper flatly did not
support. That was a warning sign, not a one-off. When a large, promising-looking
claim turned up in a second paper — a depth formula that happened to land almost
exactly on the leaderboard's target — it got the same treatment: read again,
specifically asking for the exact theorem text and the specific step that justifies
the depth bound, not a summary of the paper's conclusions. This section's most
important finding is procedural: **a summarized read of a paper is a lead, not a
result.** Only something checked against the paper's own stated reasoning, or
against this project's own direct computation, gets treated as a fact from here on.

## 3. Confirmed real, and still true after the skeptical pass

### 3.1 Graph-coloring for circuit depth is a real, current (2026), published technique

Minimizing depth for a set of *already-chosen* commuting gates provably reduces to
graph vertex-coloring: gates are nodes, non-parallelizable pairs are edges, a valid
coloring is a valid depth-minimal schedule. Re-verified with a clean fetch after the
first, unreliable summary was discarded. **What it does not do, confirmed on the
same careful re-read: it does not offer any way to make gates that are already
forced to share a qubit stop conflicting.** It schedules a given gate set; it cannot
invent parallelism that isn't already there. This directly supports Finding 8 from
the previous day (the 31-way clique in the current design) as a real, structural
limit of *that* design's gate choices — not a scheduling failure fixable by a
smarter algorithm.

### 3.2 The generic parity/Walsh-basis diagonal-unitary synthesis is a confirmed dead end

Quoted directly from the paper on a second, careful fetch: for a fully dense
n-qubit diagonal unitary with no ancilla, the achieved depth is **2ⁿ** (about a 50%
improvement over a prior 2ⁿ⁺¹−3 bound — not an asymptotic breakthrough). For our
n=12, that is depth ≈4096. This matches, independently, the direct Walsh-spectrum
computation from the previous day's findings (all 4096 coefficients of the target
function nonzero, no exploitable sparsity). Two independent methods agree: switching
to this basis outright is closed, not merely unpromising.

### 3.3 The complement (mark white pixels instead of black) is worse, not better

`plan.md` flagged this as "free to try" back on 2026-09-13; nobody had actually
measured it. Checked today with a direct GF(2) rank computation: the black mask's
rank is 10 (matching the existing design); the white mask's rank is **11**. More
terms needed for the exact style of construction this project's best result uses.
Closed.

### 3.4 The "ancilla-starved" regime is a real, named boundary in the current literature — corroborated by three independent sources, not one

This project's own numbers put us at **6 ancilla for 12 variables**. Three
independent papers, unprompted, each drew their own boundary line in almost exactly
this place:

- The `caterpillar`/pebbling tool (previous day's Finding 9) failed to find *any*
  schedule from 6 pebbles up to 36-38, on two different real networks.
- A SAT-oracle clustering paper (HRSE model) explicitly states its own technique's
  benefits "diminish" once the ancilla budget falls far below the variable count —
  precisely our regime (`aq ≪ n`).
- A general "nearly optimal Boolean oracle" paper's *sparse*-function variant (the
  one that would exploit our function only having 1097 true inputs out of 4096)
  requires `m ≥ Θ(log n + log d)` ancilla — for our n=12, d=1097, that's **≈14
  ancilla**, more than double our budget of 6. Its *general* (non-sparse) variant
  does cover m=6, but see §4.1 below for why that variant's own depth claim did not
  survive the second read.

**This is a genuine, useful, negative-shaped finding on its own:** most published
"smart scheduling" techniques assume meaningfully more ancilla relative to variable
count than this problem allows. If the leaders have an edge here, it is very likely
a technique built *specifically* for this ancilla-starved regime, not a fancier
version of a technique that needs more scratch space than we have.

## 4. Did not survive the skeptical pass

### 4.1 "Nearly Optimal Boolean Oracle Circuits" (arXiv:2607.28402), Theorem 1 — unverified, do not build on this yet

The first read found this paper's general-case formula, `depth = O(b·2ⁿ/(n+m))`,
lands at **≈228** for our exact parameters (n=12, m=6) — startlingly close to the
leaderboard's 137. That similarity is what earned this paper a careful second look,
specifically asking: *why* is the depth this small, given the construction visibly
iterates over `2^p` sequential prefix values (p≈8 in the paper's own worked example)?

The honest answer, quoting the second, careful fetch directly: **"The paper does
not adequately explain how the depth remains O(b2ⁿ/(n+m)) despite iterating over 2ᵖ
sequential terms... the proof section states each term has depth O(2^q/(p+m)) but
does not explicitly show why iterating 2ᵖ times doesn't multiply this depth."**

There is also a striking, and slightly suspicious, numerical coincidence worth
naming plainly: with `n=12, m=6`, the paper's own formula gives `q = Θ(log(n+m))`
and therefore `2^p = 2^n/(n+m) ≈ 4096/18 ≈ 227.6` — which is *the same number* as
the claimed total depth (`≈228`, since b=1). In plain terms: the claimed "depth"
bound is numerically identical to "one unit of depth per iteration," for a
construction whose own description sounds like it needs each iteration to fully
finish (compute a signature, conditionally reset qubits, compute a sub-function,
conditionally XOR it in) before the same physical qubits are reusable for the next
one. That is exactly the kind of claim that needs the actual proof, not a summary,
before anyone trusts it enough to spend implementation time on it. **Status: a real,
recent, seemingly serious paper, with a genuinely interesting resource-reuse idea
inside it (see §5.1) — but its headline depth number is not verified and should not
be treated as evidence the target is reachable this way.**

### 4.2 SSHR / parallelotope-based CNOT synthesis (arXiv:2509.01912) — downgraded after checking it against what we already know

On the first pass this looked immediately actionable: a real tool for ≤8-variable
Boolean functions, claiming 56%/81% CNOT reduction versus ESOP/XAG, and we already
have an 8-variable reduced disk-2 truth table sitting in `cpp/disk2.pla`.

On reflection, a "parallelotope embedded in the Boolean hypercube" is the same
object this project has been calling a **Cube** since day one (fix some bits, leave
others free) — and "Weighted Set Cover Problem with Parity Constraints, solved by an
ILP solver" is a more *exact* version of exactly the "greedy ESOP over all cubes"
approach `handbuilt.py`'s own docstring already records as a measured dead end
(*"greedy ESOP over all 3^12 cubes: 61 cubes, 2701 CX est"*). SSHR would very likely
find a *better* cover than that greedy search — an exact ILP solver beats a greedy
heuristic by construction — but it is optimizing *within the same algebra* that
Finding 1 (previous day) proved has a rank-10 floor for this specific target, and
that Finding 8 proved has a ~31-way qubit-sharing clique for this specific target,
for reasons tied to the target's own geometry (§3.4 of the previous day's document
and Phase 3.5's bit-sensitivity computation), not to how cleverly the cover is
chosen. Its reported 56-81% CNOT reduction is measured on the paper's own generic
small-function benchmark suite, not on a function with this specific, unusually
expensive-to-tile geometry. There is also a scoring mismatch: SSHR targets CNOT
*count*; this challenge scores *depth* first, and Finding 6 (previous day) already
showed those two objectives can pull in different directions in this exact
toolchain. **Status: plausible, bounded, cheap to sanity-check directly (see the
action plan) — but there is no strong reason, grounded in what this project has
already proven, to expect it to escape the existing ceiling.**

### 4.3 Ancilla/depth trade-off SAT-oracle paper (arXiv:2101.05430) — real numbers, no visible method, not yet actionable

Concrete, specific claims (3 ancilla with a quadratic size increase; O(log m) depth
with m ancilla) from an unreviewed preprint (arXiv only, no venue found, 2021-2022).
The abstract states results without describing the recursive construction, and no
source code was found. It targets CNF-form SAT instances specifically — applying it
here would require converting a reduced disk truth table to CNF first, which is a
real but nontrivial research step of its own, not yet attempted. **Status: worth
returning to only if there's appetite to invest in the CNF-conversion research
first; not actionable today.**

## 5. What's left standing — the two ideas worth actually trying

### 5.1 Conditionally-clean ancilla: borrow the coordinate qubits themselves as scratch

Even with Theorem 1's headline depth number unverified (§4.1), the *resource
management idea* inside it is real, well-defined independent of that number, and
has never been tried in this project: **temporarily use some of the x/y coordinate
qubits as extra scratch space mid-computation, then restore them exactly before the
circuit ends**, rather than treating them as permanently read-only (as every design
this project has built so far does). This is a genuinely different move from
anything in `findings-2026-09-15.md` — it doesn't need Theorem 1 to be correct to be
worth testing on its own, bounded terms, since it only claims "more usable scratch
than the nominal 6 ancilla," not any specific depth number.

### 5.2 An exact (not greedy) cover search on the already-reduced small disk truth tables

The bounding-box reduction from Phase 3 (previous day) already produced genuinely
small functions — disk 2's is 8 variables, disk 1's is 10 — small enough that an
exact or near-exact cover search (the actual question SSHR's ILP formulation is
answering) is computationally cheap to attempt directly, without adopting SSHR's
tool wholesale. This directly tests §4.2's skepticism instead of just asserting it:
if an exact cover search still can't beat the existing rank-decomposition result
(949/616 for disk 1) on this much smaller, easier search space, that's strong
further evidence the ceiling is structural, not a search-quality problem — closing
this specific question for good rather than leaving it as a hedge.

**Update, later the same day: run, and closed — for a sharper reason than
"couldn't beat it."** A generic MILP (`scipy.optimize.milp`, HiGHS) on disk 2's
8-variable version found its best answer (35 cubes, weight 1001) almost instantly
and then failed to improve on it at all across a full 600 seconds of real
branch-and-bound search — only the *lower bound* moved (189→211), not the answer
itself. Built and verified the found solution anyway: **depth 929, CX 631, worse
than the existing 750/470.** The search landscape here isn't merely slow to
traverse, it appears to be genuinely hard for a generic solver — the disk's
specific geometry doesn't have an easy exact answer sitting nearby the way the
SSHR paper's own (differently-shaped) generic benchmarks might. Disk 1's larger
(10-variable, ~9x more candidates) version was not run, since there's no reason to
expect a harder instance of the same solver to behave better. Full writeup in
`plan.md` Phase 3.6.

## 6. Where this leaves the project

Every idea generated from the literature search has now been checked against either
its own stated reasoning or this project's own prior direct measurements. Two
survived long enough to be worth actually building (§5.1, §5.2); two were explicitly
parked with a clear reason why (§4.2's tool, §4.3); two were confirmed closed outright
(§3.2, §3.3). **Update, 2026-09-17: both of the two that survived have now been
built, measured, and closed negative too** — see `plan.md` Phase 3.6 for the real
numbers. The exact-cover search (§5.2) found a search landscape too hard for a
generic MILP to make progress on in 10 minutes, and the one answer it did produce
was worse than what's already in hand. The conditionally-clean-ancilla idea (§5.1),
tested directly against Qiskit's own real implementation of the technique
(`synth_mcx_1_dirty_kg24`), cost 4x more depth and CX than the existing approach for
a single isolated gate — its actual advantage (fewer ancilla) doesn't convert into a
depth or CX win when ancilla isn't the bottleneck for that gate.

**So: every idea this literature search produced, across two full days of chasing
it down, sanity-checking it, and then actually building and measuring the survivors,
is now closed.** That is not a failed session — it is a session that correctly
avoided spending real implementation effort on the ideas that didn't survive
scrutiny (§4), and then still measured the two that did rather than assuming they'd
work. The honest state of the project is: the leaders' actual technique remains
unexplained, literature search has now been tried as a route to finding it and has
not produced one, and the next move that hasn't been tried is questioning a more
basic assumption about the problem itself (see the closing paragraph of `plan.md`
Phase 3.5) rather than another round of either hand-building or paper-chasing on the
same territory.
